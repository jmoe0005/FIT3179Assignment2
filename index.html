Holidays at Home

How Australians travel within their own country, and how COVID-19 changed it. An overnight trip is a trip of at least 40 km from home that includes a night away.

A collapse, then a rebound

In 2019 Australians took 117 million overnight trips at home. Lockdowns and closed state borders cut this to 82 million in 2021, a drop of 30%.

Travel bounced back quickly once borders reopened. By 2024 we were taking 115 million trips, just 2% below 2019.

Not every state bounced back

Long lockdowns hit the ACT (−46%), Victoria (−40%) and New South Wales (−38%) hardest in 2021. Western Australia fell only 11%: with its border closed, 94% of its trips that year were West Australians holidaying in their own state.

By 2024 only Queensland had more trips than before COVID. The Northern Territory was still 17% down.

Where Australians go

New South Wales receives the most trips simply because it has the most people. Dividing trips by population shows which places draw visitors beyond their size: the ACT (6.3 trips per resident), Tasmania (5.5) and the Northern Territory (5.3). Spending tells a different story: New South Wales ($31.5 billion) and Queensland ($29.0 billion) take the largest share of tourist dollars.

Why we travel

Holidays are the one reason for travel that grew past its pre-COVID level, up 6% to 49 million trips.

Trips to visit friends and relatives and business trips are both still about 7% lower than in 2019.

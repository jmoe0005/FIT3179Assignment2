Domestic tourism in Australia, 2019 to 2026

Holidays at Home

COVID-19 closed state borders and emptied our cities. Six years on, how far have we come back, where do we go, and why? An overnight trip here means a trip at least 40 km from home that includes a night away.

01 · The big picture

A collapse, then a comeback

In 2019 Australians took 117 million overnight trips at home.

Lockdowns and closed state borders cut this to 72.5 million in 2020, a fall of 38%. In 2021 trips were still 30% down.

Once borders reopened, travel bounced back fast. By 2024 we took 115 million trips, just 2% short of 2019.

Trip numbers are almost back to normal.

02 · Recovery

The big cities are still catching up

Long lockdowns hit the ACT, Victoria and New South Wales hardest. In 2021 they received about 40% fewer trips than in 2019.

Western Australia fell by only 11%. With its border shut, West Australians holidayed at home.

By 2024 only Queensland was ahead of 2019. The Northern Territory was still 17% down.

Sydney, Melbourne, Hobart and Darwin all still receive about 10% fewer trips than before COVID.

Regional Tasmania, Victoria and Queensland now receive more visitors than in 2019.

Regional Northern Territory is the exception: trips there are down by 22%.

The regions recovered faster than the cities.

03 · Destinations

Where Australians go

Big states receive the most trips simply because more people live there. To compare fairly, the map divides trips by each state's population.

Tasmania attracts the most visitors for its size: 5.1 trips for every person who lives there.

Western Australia, which is large and far from the east coast, receives the fewest: 3.2 per resident.

04 · Crossing borders

Who travels where

The busiest route links Queensland and New South Wales

Overnight trips from one state or territory to another, 2024. Choose a state to see only the trips that start there.

About one overnight trip in four crosses a state border.

The busiest route runs up and down the east coast: 4.6 million trips from Queensland to New South Wales, and almost as many the other way.

Victorians made 4.1 million trips to New South Wales and 2.4 million to Queensland.

Smaller flows that the survey could not measure reliably are left out.

Most trips in the big states are made by locals. In Western Australia, 85% of visitors live in the state.

Smaller places depend on visitors from other states. They make 64% of trips to the Northern Territory and 46% of trips to Tasmania.

In the ACT almost every overnight visitor comes from interstate, because few Canberrans travel 40 km or more within the territory.

05 · Purpose and spending

Why we travel, and where the money goes

Holidays are the only reason for travel that has grown since 2019

Overnight trips by reason for the trip, in millions, 2019 and 2024

Compared with 2019, Australians took 6% more holidays in 2024.

Trips to visit friends and relatives and business trips are both still about 7% lower.

The newest survey shows that business travel is still shrinking.

Business trips halved in just over a year, from 4.1 million in early 2025 to 2.0 million a quarter in mid-2026.

Holiday trips were highest over the 2025–26 summer.

Business trips halved between early 2025 and mid-2026

People visiting family spend half as much on accommodation

How every $100 spent on an overnight trip is split, 2024. One square = $1.

Spending items too small for the survey to measure reliably are left out.

On a holiday or a business trip, about $32 of every $100 goes on accommodation.

People visiting friends and relatives spend only $17, because many stay for free with their hosts. They spend more on food and on getting there instead.

Business travellers spend the most on transport: $40 of every $100, mostly on flights.

In short

What has changed

We travel as much as before COVID, but each trip costs about 40% more.

Most regions have recovered. Sydney, Melbourne, Hobart and Darwin are still about 10% down.

Holidays are growing, while business travel keeps shrinking.

Notes on the data

  • Trips and spending cover Australians aged 15 and over (14 and over from 2025).
  • 2020 appears only in the national totals (section 1). The state and regional tables used here start in 2019 and resume in 2021.
  • From 2025, Tourism Research Australia uses a new survey method, so 2025 and 2026 figures are not directly comparable with earlier years.
  • Dollar figures are not adjusted for inflation.
